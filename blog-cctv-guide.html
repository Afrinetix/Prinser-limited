How to Choose CCTV Cameras for Home Security in Kenya

Everything you need to know before buying and installing a CCTV security system for your home or business in Kenya.

Security is a growing concern for homeowners and business owners across Kenya. Whether you're in Nairobi, Mombasa, or Machakos, a well-designed CCTV system can significantly deter crime and give you peace of mind. But with so many options available, choosing the right CCTV cameras can feel overwhelming.

At Prinser Limited in Machakos, we've installed over 500 CCTV systems across homes, shops, offices, schools, and institutions. In this guide, we share the key factors you need to consider before making your purchase.

1. Understand Your Security Needs First

Before buying any camera, answer these questions:

  • What areas do you want to cover? (Perimeter, entrance, parking, interior?)
  • How many cameras do you need?
  • Is this for a home, shop, office, or institution?
  • Do you need remote viewing on your phone?
  • What is your budget?

A professional site assessment - which Prinser Limited offers free of charge - answers all these questions and helps design the right system for your specific property.

2. Camera Types: Which One Do You Need?

Bullet Cameras

Cylinder-shaped cameras ideal for long-distance outdoor monitoring. Easy to aim at a specific area like a driveway or gate. Highly visible - great deterrent effect.

Best for: Gates, perimeter walls, driveways

Dome Cameras

Circular, vandal-resistant cameras. The dome shape makes it hard for criminals to tell which direction the camera is facing - a strong psychological deterrent.

Best for: Indoor use, shops, offices, corridors

PTZ Cameras

Pan-Tilt-Zoom cameras that can rotate 360° and zoom in on specific areas. Controlled remotely from your phone or monitoring station.

Best for: Large open areas, parking lots, institutions

IP / Wi-Fi Cameras

Network-connected cameras that transmit footage over Wi-Fi or ethernet. Easy to add to an existing network. Excellent for remote monitoring via mobile app.

Best for: Homes with Wi-Fi, small businesses

3. Resolution: How Clear Do You Need the Footage?

Camera resolution determines how clear your footage will be. Here's a quick guide for Kenyan buyers:

  • 720p (HD): Basic - sufficient for general monitoring but limited for identifying faces. Not recommended for new installations.
  • 1080p (Full HD): The standard choice. Clear enough to identify individuals, read number plates, and provide useful evidence. This is what Prinser Limited recommends for most installations.
  • 4K (Ultra HD): Premium option with exceptional detail. Ideal for large premises where you need to zoom in on footage. Higher storage requirements.
"We always recommend at minimum 1080p Full HD cameras for new installations in Kenya. 720p cameras may be cheaper upfront but they often fail to provide useful evidence when you need it most."

4. Night Vision: Essential in Kenya

Most crime in Kenya happens at night or in low-light conditions. This makes night vision a non-negotiable feature. Look for:

  • Infrared (IR) Night Vision: Standard feature that provides black-and-white footage in the dark. Range varies from 20m to 60m+ depending on the camera. Adequate for most home installations.
  • Full-Color Night Vision: More advanced cameras use a combination of low-light sensors and external light to produce colour footage at night. Better for identification but costs more.
  • Starlight Technology: Premium cameras that can produce clear colour images in near-total darkness. Recommended for high-security applications.

5. DVR vs NVR: What's the Difference?

  • DVR (Digital Video Recorder): Works with analogue cameras connected via coaxial cables. Suitable for budget installations and upgrading existing analogue systems.
  • NVR (Network Video Recorder): Works with IP cameras over ethernet or Wi-Fi. Higher resolution support, more flexible installation, and generally better image quality. This is the preferred modern choice.

At Prinser Limited, we recommend NVR systems with IP cameras for all new installations due to their superior performance and scalability.

6. Storage: How Much Do You Need?

CCTV footage requires significant storage. Here's a rough guide:

  • 4 cameras, 1080p, 7 days storage: ~1TB hard drive
  • 8 cameras, 1080p, 14 days storage: ~2–4TB hard drive
  • 16 cameras, 4K, 30 days storage: ~8TB+ hard drive

Motion-triggered recording can significantly reduce storage requirements by only recording when movement is detected. We configure this during installation.

7. Remote Viewing: Monitor Anywhere in Kenya

Modern CCTV systems allow you to watch live footage and review recordings from your smartphone - anywhere in the world, as long as you have internet. At Prinser Limited, we configure this during installation and set up the app on your phone. Brands like Hikvision (Hik-Connect app) and Dahua (gDMSS) have reliable and free mobile apps.

8. Choosing a Reliable Brand in Kenya

The Kenyan market is flooded with cheap, unbranded cameras that fail quickly. We always recommend established brands:

  • Hikvision: World's #1 CCTV brand. Excellent reliability, wide range, strong local support network in Kenya.
  • Dahua: Premium cameras with excellent image quality. Great for business and institutional use.
  • Uniview: Mid-range brand offering excellent value without compromising quality.
